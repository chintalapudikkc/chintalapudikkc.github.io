About Me

I am a Senior Principal Researcher at Microsoft M365 Research, where I am the tech lead and architect for end-to-end inference efficiency of agentic workflows: workload characterization, orchestrator–inference engine co-design, KV-cache management and efficient attention.

My recent AI work includes task-specific LLM compression (LLM-Sieve: 20–75% parameter reduction at ≤1–5% accuracy loss) and prospection-guided agentic memory (EMNLP 2026), which introduced the public MemoryQuest benchmark.

Machine learning has run through my whole career. I started with neural-network pruning and feature selection (1997–99), and my best-known systems work on indoor localization (EZ, Zee, Centaur) is built on Bayesian inference, particle filters and custom optimization. Over 20 years at Microsoft Research and Bosch I have turned research into shipped systems, including Azure ML labeling (~10× cost reduction) and the next-generation Xbox controller protocol (5× lower latency).

I have published 50+ peer-reviewed papers with 9,500+ citations, hold 30+ US patents, and received the ACM Test of Time Award in 2022. I have mentored researchers who are now faculty at UIUC, Cornell, NYU, Georgia Tech and UC Irvine, and served on the FCC Technological Advisory Council AI Working Group (2022–23).

Background

I joined Microsoft M365 Research in 2026. Before that I was a researcher at Microsoft Research Redmond (2016–2026), Microsoft Research India (2009–2016), and the Bosch Research and Technology Center in Palo Alto, CA (2005–2009).

I graduated from the University of Southern California with a PhD in Computer Science in 2006, advised by Prof. Ramesh Govindan. Before my PhD I obtained a Masters in Electrical Engineering (Signal Processing and Control Systems) from Drexel University and a B.Tech in Electrical Engineering from IIT (BHU) Varanasi.
